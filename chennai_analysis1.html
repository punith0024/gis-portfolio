4. Analytical Discussion & Interpretation

Spatial Distribution of Atmospheric Carbon Monoxide

The 2020 annual mean Sentinel-5P tropospheric carbon monoxide (CO) column density across Chennai averages 0.039115 mol/m², with the upper quartile (75th percentile) boundary established at 0.039309 mol/m². Areas falling in this top quartile span 49.95 km², or 25.50% of the administrative district.

Spatially, the highest CO column burdens are clustered over the northern and north-western industrial belts (Ennore, Manali, and Thiruvottiyur) and major inland freight transit corridors. These sectors host thermal energy generation facilities, petrochemical refineries, fertilizer plants, and heavy diesel freight servicing the ports. In contrast, eastern coastal sectors exhibit lower column densities due to persistent maritime boundary layer circulation and sea-breeze dispersion into the Bay of Bengal.

Temporal Dynamics (Monthly Variation)

The monthly dynamic sequence illustrates marked seasonal shifts across 2020:

  • Winter Pre-Monsoon (Jan–Feb): High column accumulations due to shallow nocturnal planetary boundary layer heights that suppress vertical mixing.
  • Lockdown Contraction (Mar–May): Reductions in tropospheric column densities following COVID-19 national lockdowns that curtailed vehicular and commercial operations.
  • Monsoon Dilution & Scavenging (Jun–Dec): Strong maritime wind advection and precipitation scavenging significantly lower observable column densities, followed by industrial and transit recovery toward late 2020.

Demographic Exposure Patterns (Residents Aged 65+)

According to WorldPop 2020 age-sex estimates, residents aged 65 and above account for 286,997 individuals (7.33% of the city total). Among them, 79,662 elderly individuals reside within the city's highest-CO quartile zone, representing an exposure share of 27.76%.

The exposure share of the elderly cohort (27.76%) closely parallels that of the total population (27.77%), both exceeding the pure spatial area share of 25.50%. This indicates that the upper quartile is not restricted to vacant industrial tracts, but overlaps dense residential wards. Given age-associated reductions in cardiopulmonary reserve and higher prevalence of chronic vascular conditions, older adults residing in these zones face amplified environmental health risks.

Methodological & Data Limitations

  • Column Density vs. Ground-Level Exposure: Sentinel-5P TROPOMI measures vertically integrated total atmospheric column density (mol/m²). It represents total gas between the ground and the top of the atmosphere, not surface breathing-zone concentrations (ppm or µg/m³). Results must strictly be interpreted as residence within areas of high satellite-observed atmospheric column levels, not personal physiological dosage.
  • Spatial Resolution Mismatch: Sentinel-5P operates at a coarser native footprint (~5.5 × 3.5 km, gridded to ~1.1 km), whereas WorldPop data is disaggregated to 100 m grids. This spatial disparity assumes uniform atmospheric concentrations across individual 1.1 km cells, smoothing over street-level microclimates and localized street canyon effects.
  • Cloud Screening & Seasonal Retrieval Bias: TROPOMI optical measurements require clear-sky conditions. Persistent cloud cover during Chennai's Northeast Monsoon (October–December) results in higher pixel rejection rates, potentially causing under-sampling during wet months.
